\section{Unfolding}
\label{sec:unfolding}

The binning of the analysis in $Q2,x,z,$ and $\pTh$ was selected to have an acceptable purity and acceptance in almost all bins. The purity is defined as $N_{\mathrm{stay}}/N_{\mathrm{rec}}$, where $N_{\mathrm{rec}}$ includes the fake column. The acceptance is defined as $N_{\mathrm{rec}}/N_{\mathrm{gen}}$.

The response used for these metrics is built from RAPGAP31 with the nominal
selection: the detector-interaction-corrected response together with a
reconstruction-level veto on tracks within $\alpha<0.2$ of the scattered
electron, applied after the electron and V0 truth vetoes and before the $z$ guard.  The
generator side is unchanged; matched tracks rejected by this veto enter the miss row.
The current-hemisphere requirements are $xF_{\mathrm{SIDIS\_es}}<0$ at
reconstruction level and $xFGen<0$ at generator level; generator-level $x_F$
is recomputed with the photon four-momentum $q$ built from the nominal beam energies.  The reconstructed
$xF_{\mathrm{SIDIS\_es}}$ keeps the electron-like photon: it uses the electron-method
$\Qsq$ and angle, not the $e\Sigma$ photon, so only $z$ and $\pTh$ follow
$e\Sigma$ (a stated limitation). The prior of the iterative Bayesian
unfolding (IBU) is the response's own generator-level truth distribution,
computed from this response.

The worst purities are found in the higher $x$ strata at high $\pTh$, where the minimum value is 3.0\%. At high-$x$, the resolution on the virtual photon is generally worse, resulting in large migrations from the much more populated low-$\pTh$ region to the high-$\pTh$ tail. The systematic uncertainties on these bins are very large, and they may be dropped from the final set of published bins. The purities for $h^+$ and $h^-$ are shown in Figs.~\ref{fig:unfolding:alpha0p2-purity} and~\ref{fig:unfolding:alpha0p2-purity-hm}, respectively. The worst acceptances are found in the low-$z$, high $\pTh$ region, where the tracks predominantly fall at $\eta>1.6$ in the lab frame.

The nominal unfolding method is the D'Agostini-style IBU implemented in RooUnfold, using $k=4$ iterations; bin-by-bin correction is retained as a cross-check. TUnfold is presented as a diagnostic comparison only; it does not provide the central result. Both methods carry uncertainties associated with the model-dependence of the migration matrix, limited data and/or MC statistics, and the choice of arbitrary parameters ($\tau$ for TUnfold and $N_{\mathrm{iter.}}$ for IBU). The uncertainties will be described in more detail in Sec.~\ref{sec:systematics}.

For the diagnostic TUnfold comparison, the migration matrix is produced with twice the number of reconstruction-level bins in $\pTh$ to help the unfolding better deal with migrations. To reduce the size of the matrix, $h^+$ and $h^-$ are unfolded separately, only 0.19\% of tracks are reconstructed with the wrong charge, so this is a reasonable approximation\footnote{This should be compared to the 2020 analysis, where each kinematic bin was unfolded separately.}. Charge-confused tracks are treated as misses in one matrix and fakes in the other. $\pTh$ was chosen as the variable to bin more finely since it experiences the largest migration. Unfolding with larger numbers ($>2\times$) of reconstruction-level bins gave similar results at the cost of significantly larger compute time. The value of the regularization strength, $\tau$, in TUnfold was chosen to minimize correlations between bins as well as the model, statistical, and regularization uncertainties. The model uncertainty is defined as the difference between the data unfolded with the Rapgap migration matrix and the data unfolded with the Djangoh migration matrix. One challenge with TUnfold is that in the high-$\pTh$ bins where data statistics are low, some bins occasionally unfold to a negative cross section.

The Bayesian unfolding does a good job of reproducing the truth of Rapgap when the reconstruction-level Rapgap data is unfolded with the Djangoh migration matrix, and vice versa. It also provides stable results for most bins at any value of $N_{\mathrm{iter.}}<10$. The two methods generally agree quite well, with the exception of some edge bins where TUnfold produces oscillations.

Figure~\ref{fig:unfolding:alpha0p2-allfake-migration-matrix} shows the diagnostic all-fake response used for the provisional near-electron opening-angle veto study. The positive- and negative-charge response matrices each have shape $(491,246)$: rows 0--489 are physical reconstructed bins and row 490 is the miss row, while columns 0--244 are physical truth bins and column 245 is the fake column. The response is shown without stretching of the fake or miss bins and is column-normalized as $P(\mathrm{reco\ bin}\mid\mathrm{truth\ bin})$ on a logarithmic colour scale. This all-fake response is diagnostic and provisional only; the central response and the final cross sections are unchanged.

The cross-generator closure diagnostic of the unfolding is shown in Figs.~\ref{fig:unfolding:ibu-crossclosure-fullrange} and~\ref{fig:unfolding:ibu-crossclosure-zoom}. These plots use the canonical seven-column $\pTh$ layout and include both cross directions, with $\mathrm{DJ}\to\mathrm{RG}=N^{\mathrm{DJ\ reco,\ unfolded\ with\ RG\ response}}/N^{\mathrm{DJ\ truth}}$ and $\mathrm{RG}\to\mathrm{DJ}=N^{\mathrm{RG\ reco,\ unfolded\ with\ DJ\ response}}/N^{\mathrm{RG\ truth}}$. The comparison is a cross-generator closure check only, with no self-closure component; the full-range version displays ratios from 0 to 2 and the zoomed version displays 0.75 to 1.25. These diagnostics are derived from the validated cross-closure results.

\subsection{Generator-Level Definitions Shared with the Radiative Correction}
\label{sec:unfolding:truth-contract}

The response and the radiative correction share one set of generator-level
definitions.  The matched body of the response assigns its generator-level
$\Qsq$ bin with $Q^2_{\mathrm{esGen}}$, the event kinematics evaluated with
the nominal beam energies (i.e.\ at the radiative level).  This matches the
generator-level binning of the pool and the miss row, and the binning
$(Q^2_{\mathrm{esGen}}, x_{\mathrm{es,gen}}, z, \pTh)$ of the radiative
correction: the response mirrors exactly the binning in which
$C_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is derived and applied
(Sec.~\ref{sec:radiative_corrections:application}).  Before this
harmonization the matched body was binned with the radiation-safe estimator
$Q^2_{\mathrm{es,gen}}$, while the pool, the miss row, and the
radiative-correction samples were binned with $Q^2_{\mathrm{esGen}}$.  Under
initial-state radiation $Q^2_{\mathrm{esGen}}$ always exceeds the
photon-subtracted $Q^2_{\mathrm{es,gen}}$, so generator entries of radiative
events crossed bin boundaries upward between the body and the pool.  This
left a bias of definite sign in each bin of the earlier cross sections; the
harmonization removes it as a one-time correction of the central values.

Everything else in the response construction is unchanged by the
harmonization.  This covers the pool and miss binning (already
$Q^2_{\mathrm{esGen}}$), the generator-level $z$, the generator-level $\pTh$
(nominal-beam construction), the definition of $x$, the
$\alpha\ge0.2~\mathrm{rad}$ cone, the detector-interaction treatment, the
track matching and event-count bookkeeping, the entire reconstructed side,
and the weights.  The radiation-safe generator-level fiducial selection is
also unchanged: $Q^2_{\mathrm{es,gen}}$, $Y_{\mathrm{es,gen}}$,
$x_{\mathrm{es,gen}}$, the $E-p_z>45~\GeV$ requirement, and the
current-hemisphere cut recomputed with the nominal beam energies.  The pool,
miss, and prior entries use the weight $w_{1}w_{2}$.  The matched-body and
fake reconstructed entries use $w_{1}w_{2}$ times the per-track correction
for hadron loss through nuclear interactions in the detector material,
applied exactly once and clipped to $[0,10]$.  The background-subtracted
data are used unchanged.  The applied DJANGOH14 radiative-correction table
required no re-derivation: it was verified to be consistent with these
definitions and identical to the table used before.

The generator-level $z$ convention is kept and stated explicitly.  Keeping
the nominal-beam generator-level $z$ and $\pTh$ leaves the $z$ and $\pTh$
axes exactly matched to the radiative correction, and this $z$ is the
correct truth definition under the shared definitions.  In a single-slice MC
study, $3.25\%$ of generator-level rows ($3.4\%$ weighted) would change $z$
bin under the radiation-safe definition of $z$, all upward and concentrated
in the two lowest $z$ rows.  This shift of $z$ bin is carried as a declared
bin-coordinate convention.  Its systematic treatment is the
``Initial-state-radiation $z$ shift (zero)'' term of the uncertainty budget,
which is identically zero; its residual from initial-state radiation is not
quoted (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:budget-regeneration}).

The harmonized response was validated with a full-sample study of the
per-bin straddle rate (the fraction of generator entries that cross a bin
boundary) and of the matched fraction, over the complete response sample (73
RAPGAP31 and 101 DJANGOH14 input files).  An independent check confirmed that
the pattern of per-bin shifts is physically consistent.  The study accounts
exactly for both generator samples: 1{,}357{,}250 RAPGAP31 and 1{,}574{,}230
DJANGOH14 body-bin changes, checked against 58{,}768{,}556 and 69{,}695{,}815
rows.  Three benign mismatches at $\pTh$ bin edges occur in the RAPGAP31
sample and none in the DJANGOH14 sample; the overall unweighted straddle
rates are $1.8\%$ (RAPGAP31) and $1.7\%$ (DJANGOH14).  The per-bin unfolded
shifts are consistent with the removal of the binning bias: their sign
pattern, magnitudes, charge symmetry, and near-conservation of the integral
all agree with the mechanism.  Every well-populated bin shift is bounded by
its full-sample per-bin straddle rate; the largest populated shift,
$+4.2\%$ (negative charge, global bin 159), is $0.855$ of its own rate.

The 48 cells flagged as exceptions were each examined.  Of these, 44 sit in
two near-balanced cells where the in- and out-flux nearly cancel (a
small-denominator artifact).  The three corner bins b39, b109, and b179 are
dominated by noise: their shifts are driven by redistribution of the prior in
near-empty bins, and the shifts have opposite signs for the two charges,
which rules out a coherent systematic effect.  No bin is therefore masked,
and no further treatment is required.  The
acceptance window of the fake-track removal check was re-derived on the
updated fake-track fractions (centres $11.3\%$ for $\hplus$ and $10.9\%$ for
$\hminus$), and the values measured on the background-subtracted data fall
inside the re-derived windows.

The response and closure figures of this section predate the harmonization
and are retained as diagnostics; they have not been regenerated on the
harmonized response.

\subsection{Cross-check with TUnfold}
\label{sec:unfolding:tunfold-crosscheck}

TUnfold, as implemented in ROOT, is retained as the validated second-method cross-check.  The iterative Bayesian unfolding, with the response's own generator-level prior and four iterations, is the final nominal method; the TUnfold result is diagnostic only.  The comparison uses the earlier detector-interaction-corrected basis without the electron cone, at raw level, which is also the basis of the existing figures in this section.  This superseded observable basis is retained for the diagnostic comparison; the comparison has not yet been repeated on the current basis.

For TUnfold, $\tau=10^{-5}$ was selected by requiring an absolute bias below 1\% for both generators.  The delivered solution is $\max(x,0)$; its one-sided clipping bias, $+0.028\%$ for $h^+$ and $+0.011\%$ for $h^-$, is accounted for separately.  In a 1000-toy validation on identical toys, both methods were unbiased and the TUnfold statistical uncertainty was toy-validated, with toy-to-reported ratios of 0.975 and 0.969 for $h^+$ and $h^-$, respectively.  Propagating the covariance within the TUnfold solution (TUnfoldSys) gave median total uncertainties of 9.2\% and 10\%, compared with 8.2\% and 8.4\% in the nominal uncertainty budget; the origin of the excess is documented in the validation study.
