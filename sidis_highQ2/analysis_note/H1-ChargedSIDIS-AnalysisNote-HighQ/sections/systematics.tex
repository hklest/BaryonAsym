\section{Systematic Uncertainties}
\label{sec:systematics}

The systematic uncertainties of this measurement comprise detector, unfolding,
tracking-efficiency, hadron-azimuth, luminosity, radiative, and
background-subtraction components, evaluated on all 490 bins.  They use the
near-electron cone selection with the response corrected for secondary
interactions in the detector, the subtraction of the electron, V0, and
low-$\Qsq$ background templates before unfolding, and the virtual photon
reconstructed with the $e\Sigma$ method for $z$, $\pTh$, and the $\gamma^{*}p$
frame.  The unfolded, background-subtracted data are the single denominator
for every relative component.  Over the 245 bins per charge the median total
uncertainty is $8.3\%$ for $\hplus$ and $8.9\%$ for $\hminus$.  All relative values are fractions of the
cross section, so the same percentage holds for the raw and for the Born
table; the budget is built against the raw cross-section tables.  Global covariance
matrices are provided (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:global-covariance}).  The radiative correction
$C_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is applied to the cross section at Born level, with its
uncertainty carried as the two systematic components described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:radiative_corrections} and listed in
Table~\ref{tab:syst:z005-components}.  The remaining open item for the
radiative correction is its application at the multiplicity level.

\subsection{Budget with the $e\Sigma$ Method}
\label{sec:syst:budget-regeneration}

With the $e\Sigma$ method the electron-energy lever arm is reduced, because
$z$ and $\pTh$ no longer follow the photon reconstructed from the electron alone.
For the electron-only $q$, a $1\%$ shift of $E_{e'}$
moves $z$ by about $11\%$ at $y_e\approx0.09$ and by $1.3\%$ at $y_e\approx0.45$, and the
median of the electron-energy term with the electron-only photon is $4.1\%$ ($\hplus$) and
$4.2\%$ ($\hminus$), against $2.7\%$ and $2.9\%$ with the $e\Sigma$ photon.
The changes with respect to the budget with the electron-only photon, and their limits, are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the new hadronic $E-p_z$ resolution term, a smear of the hadronic $\Sigma_h$ applied in
        simulation only, from a single draw, with a noise level, estimated as the median
        $|\mathrm{RAPGAP}-\mathrm{DJANGOH}|/2$ (a generator-difference proxy, not a
        repeat-draw spread), of $0.17\%$ ($\hplus$) and $0.14\%$ ($\hminus$);
  \item the hadronic energy scale (JES) term is taken from RAPGAP only and symmetrized as
        $(\mathrm{up}-\mathrm{down})/2$, because the generator test gives
        $p=1.3\times10^{-6}$ for the RAPGAP--DJANGOH disagreement; the DJANGOH
        difference is stated, not added, and the test ignores bin correlations;
  \item the secondary-interaction removal term is taken from the analysis with the
        electron-only photon (median $0.03\%$, not recomputed with the $e\Sigma$ method), and a $\pm100\%$
        normalization term is added to it;
  \item the hadron azimuthal-angle prior term is evaluated with the $e\Sigma$ method;
  \item there is no ISR/QED term: following standard H1 practice, radiative effects
        are corrected with $C_{\mathrm{rad}}$, which is applied, and the ISR residual
        of the initial-state-radiation $z$-shift term, itself identically zero, is not quoted;
  \item a hadronic-final-state $E-p_z$ systematic variation is still missing: the
        hadronic-final-state shifts leave $E-p_z$ bit-identical, so the quoted
        hadronic-final-state uncertainties do not cover the $E-p_z>45~\GeV$ requirement.
\end{itemize}

The five detector components (scattered-electron energy and polar angle, and
HFS JES, RCES, and polar angle) are full-chain MC-only variations: the
shift is applied at the reconstructed electron or HFS in the simulation and
propagated through the shifted response and the unfolding $\mathcal{U}$ of the nominal data,
$\Delta=\mathcal{U}(D_{\mathrm{nom}};M_{\mathrm{shift}})-
\mathcal{U}(D_{\mathrm{nom}};M_{\mathrm{nom}})$, with RAPGAP31 governing; the
background templates are subtracted from the data before unfolding.  For the
scattered-electron energy component
the shift direction with the larger robust median is used per charge; the
hadronic energy scale is the exception: it is taken from RAPGAP only and symmetrized
as $(\mathrm{up}-\mathrm{down})/2$.  The tracking-efficiency component
remains a data-side conservative shift, with medians $0.51\%$
($\hplus$) and $0.51\%$ ($\hminus$) on the background-subtracted denominator.  The
applied $C_{\mathrm{rad}}$ table is not recomputed with the $e\Sigma$ method, and its two
radiative components enter with the medians of Table~\ref{tab:syst:z005-components}.

The shifts applied to the HFS primarily enter the measurement through the calculation of $E-P_z$. They do not affect the tracks.

The electron and V0 background templates used in the pre-unfold subtraction
are MC-derived and their normalization
could not be measured by the dedicated control-region fits: in the
$|\mathrm{DCA}'|>0.25~\mathrm{cm}$ tail, the two-class model is
unidentifiable, with the V0 normalization at its lower bound and the electron
normalization drifting to $2.5$--$3.5$ in the failed groups, while five of the
eight fits fail and the accepted groups remain compatible with unity.  In the
analysis with the electron-only photon, the nominal subtraction corresponds to $7.3\%$ of the data after the cone selection, composed
of $3.9\%$ electron and $3.4\%$ V0 content.  It is therefore assigned the
fully correlated $\pm100\%$ envelope
$\delta_{\mathrm{bg}}=(e_{\mathrm{avg}}+V0_{\mathrm{avg}})/\mathrm{data}_{\mathrm{sub}}'$,
which replaces the earlier template-statistics term.  The tightened-selection
check, in which $|\mathrm{DCA}'|<0.3~\mathrm{cm}$ is propagated through
data, response, templates, and unfolding, finds 87 shifts above $3\sigma$
where 1.2 are expected from statistical fluctuations; its integrated Born
changes, $-2.4\%$ ($\hplus$) and $-1.4\%$ ($\hminus$), require correlated
content uncertainties of $31\%$ and $18\%$, respectively.  In the
per-bin requirement study, 23 significant shifts require more than $30\%$,
10 require more than $50\%$, and the largest requirement is $97\%$; every
significant shift is contained by the $\pm100\%$ envelope.  The earlier DCA
scan is a diagnostic study of the far tail and does not enter the quoted
budget; crediting even its one-sided tightening does not remove the need for
the envelope.  The DCA removal itself rejects $200\%$--$300\%$ more tracks
than the associated template background, so the residual tail excess is of
unknown composition and can include displaced signal.  The residual risk is
therefore recorded rather than interpreted as a background measurement.  In the
quoted budget the envelope enters as the term ``Background: normalisation'', with
medians $3.6\%$ ($\hplus$) and $3.7\%$ ($\hminus$) and 95th percentiles
$8.8\%$ and $8.9\%$.
The fully correlated treatment is retained across bins and charges.  It does
not remove the standing caveats on the low-$\Qsq$ template scale, the
hadron-loss proxy for the fake-track fraction, or the still unevaluated DIFFVM and photoproduction
background contributions; the near-electron cone remains motivated by the previous H1
charged-particle measurement~\cite{H1:2013qsm}.

\subsection{Fractional Covariance Matrices}
\label{sec:syst:global-covariance}

Cross-section covariance matrices are provided, in $\mathrm{nb}^2$ with fractional counterparts, on the 245 published bins per
charge (490 bins, charge-major order).  They are constructed for the current
observable basis, with the near-electron cone selection, the $e\Sigma$ photon,
and the electron, V0, and low-$\Qsq$ backgrounds subtracted from the data before
unfolding.  Every nonzero term of the 24-term budget appears (the identically zero $z$-shift term is omitted), with two named
replacements: the fake-track correction MC statistics are carried inside the full
response-replica MC-statistical covariance (200 replicas), and the
secondary-interaction removal is split into its model, statistical, and
normalization parts.

Each term has a declared correlation form.  The detector, hadronic $E-p_z$ resolution,
unfolding model and regularization, secondary-interaction model, radiative-correction model,
background normalisation, hadron-loss, and luminosity terms are rank-one
and fully correlated across bins, with signs from the signed MC-only shift
vectors where they exist (JES symmetrized $(\mathrm{up}-\mathrm{down})/2$).
The non-primary term is rank-one per charge and uncorrelated across charges, plus a
STR-mu rank-one piece across charges.  The bin-edge migration residual is a $2\times2$
$\hplus/\hminus$ block per generator bin.
The $\pm100\%$ secondary-interaction normalization term is rank-one and fully
correlated across bins and charges with a uniform negative sign; this form
replaces the earlier diagonal one.  The sign is inferred, not
read from a per-bin signed vector: the sum over bins of the per-bin magnitudes
equals the magnitude of the signed integrated increment, $0.033~\mathrm{nb}$
($\hplus$) and $0.028~\mathrm{nb}$ ($\hminus$), which holds only if all bins share
that sign.  The tracking, hadron-azimuth, radiative-statistics, and
background-subtraction model, stage, and fake-fraction terms are bin-diagonal, as in
the previous version, because no validated inter-bin correlation model exists for them.
The statistical covariance combines the per-charge Poisson blocks with a
cross-charge block from 1000 joint event-cluster toys.  Four rank-one signs
(unfolding model and regularization, secondary-interaction model, radiative-correction model)
are carried from the previous version.

All validation checks pass, including the reproduction of the
budget terms and the diagonal of the total covariance, which equals the squared
budget to a relative deviation of $3.3\times10^{-16}$ after the two named
replacements.  The statistical covariance has a minimum eigenvalue of
$-2.6\times10^{-6}$, at the edge of its tolerance, and the joint-toy
clipping distorts the correlations in 42 reconstructed bins; both are flagged
and left uncorrected.  The covariance provides the declared correlation model only and
no correlation model for the bin-diagonal terms.  Any use must state the
Born-versus-raw level of the nominal cross section used to convert fractional
covariance to cross-section units.

\subsection{Detector and Track-Selection Robustness Cross-Checks}
\label{sec:syst:cut-variations}

In addition to the systematic components included in the nominal budget, several
detector and track-selection variants have been performed as robustness checks
rather than central results. Ideally, the measurement would be insensitive to
small changes in detector-level cuts. We study the effect of varying the $E-p_z$
cut, the minimum lab-frame $p_T$, the $\eta$ cut, and the cut on the distance of
closest approach, generating a new migration matrix with these reconstructed-level
cuts and then repeating the unfolding on the data. The results are
shown as the ratio of the unfolded cross section with the nominal cut to the
measured cross section with the varied cut for all bins in
\cref{fig:syst:cutvar-empz25,fig:syst:cutvar-labpt,fig:syst:cutvar-eta,fig:syst:cutvar-dca1,fig:syst:cutvar-dca3}. The uncertainties on the points in these figures show the model, statistical, and regularization uncertainties of the unfolding.
These variants were produced with an earlier version of the analysis, before
the coherent unfolding prior, the secondary-interaction correction, and the
near-electron cone selection were adopted; they are retained as robustness checks of the cut scheme and
have not been repeated with the current selection.

The loosening or tightening of the DCA cut is observed to have essentially no impact on the result. The lab $p_T$ cut has a 3-4\% effect on the lowest $\pTh$ bins, with one bin (with huge statistical uncertainties) exhibiting a 10\% shift. The lab $\eta$ cut has the most significant impact, due to the fact that it removes a much larger number of particles. A few bins are reduced to having low statistics after imposing the $|\eta|<1.4$ cut, so large fluctuations in the unfolded data in these bins can be expected. As before, these bins generally already have a large systematic and statistical uncertainty. Even in the worse case, all bins lie within 2 sigma of the unvaried results.

\subsection{Near-Electron Opening-Angle Diagnostic}
\label{sec:syst:near-electron-alpha}
The previous H1 results (Refs.~\cite{H1:2013qsm,H1:2020zpd}) use a track--scattered-electron angle requirement larger than 0.2 rad to suppress anomalous tracks appearing near the scattered electron. These tracks could arise from photon conversions of final-state radiation (at the vertex or in detector material) off the electron, or tracks from the scattered electron which are mis-identified as not belonging to the scattered electron~\cite{Grebenyuk:2012thesis}. In the latter case, it would be expected that the track energy would be relatively close to the scattered electron energy measured in the calorimeter. The reconstructed momentum of the tracks very near to the scattered electron does not correlate strongly to the scattered electron energy, supporting the photon conversion hypothesis over the electron track mis-ID hypothesis.

The
$\alpha\ge0.2~\mathrm{rad}$ laboratory-frame cone on the reconstructed tracks is
part of the central selection.  The
cone is applied after the electron and V0 truth vetoes and before the
matched-fake classification; matched tracks rejected by the cone enter the
response as misses, and the coherent prior is computed from the response variant
that carries both the cone selection and the detector-interaction correction.
The diagnostic ratios in
Table~\ref{tab:syst:alpha-veto-integrated} were produced before the cone was
adopted and are retained as motivation for the cone requirement.
